:::tip[At a glance]

- **What it is:** the testbed 3GPP SA4 uses to obtain the traffic characteristics of AI media services in its 6G media study, TR 26.870 (study item FS_6G_MED, Release 20).
- **What it does:** runs reproducible AI service scenarios (chat, agents, image generation, multimodal analysis, video understanding, real-time text and audio, web search, computer use) over emulated network conditions, captures the traffic, and computes traffic, response-time, service and task metrics.
- **Where it is specified:** TR 26.870 describes the testbed in annex B, defines its scenarios, network profiles and metrics in annex D, and reports its methodology, results and conclusions in annex C.
- **Status:** TR 26.870 is a draft (V0.6.1, September 2026). Its results are initial, and several items are marked for further study.

:::

## Why it exists

The traffic characteristics of media services are relevant to the design of 6G radio and service architectures. SA4 already documents the traffic of conversational, streaming and immersive media services in TR 26.925. AI media services are new, so the 6G media study collects their traffic characteristics with a framework of its own: a testbed, a set of test scenarios, network profiles and metrics, and a methodology for drawing conclusions.

The questions come from 6G radio and system design: whether AI traffic is uplink heavy; whether it comes in data bursts with varying delay requirements within one application; whether round-trip delay matters; whether its characteristics vary strongly within one application; and what "tokenized traffic" implies. TR 26.870 records the results as the basis for its statements on AI media services. Like TR 26.925, it describes traffic observed at the IP and application layers and defines no requirements, procedures or protocols.

## How it works

- **Orchestrator:** coordinates scenario runs, applies network profiles, handles retries and generates reports.
- **Scenarios:** each a named, reproducible AI interaction pattern, defined by its input and output modalities, interaction style, protocol, and AI model or provider.
- **Clients:** adapters for commercial providers and for self-hosted models through vLLM.
- **Network emulator:** applies a network profile with Linux tc and netem, on egress and, through an IFB device, on ingress.
- **Capture and analysis:** L3/L4 packet capture with tcpdump, optional L7 capture with mitmproxy, logging to SQLite, metric computation and plots.

A test run combines a scenario, a network profile and an execution mode, and produces a metric record. In **remote execution** the model runs at a public cloud provider and the traffic crosses the main network interface. In **local execution** the model is self-hosted next to the client, and the impairment is applied on the loopback path to emulate a radio link to a local edge server. Each combination is run several times, to obtain meaningful results.

See [Test design](./scope) for the scenarios and network profiles, and [Metrics](./metrics) for what is measured.

## What it has found

TR 26.870 states initial conclusions for each of the five questions. The figures behind them are on [Results](./results).

- **Uplink heavy:** for some scenarios. Uplink dominates where a high bit-rate modality such as video is sent for inference and a low bit-rate result returns, and in the agentic scenarios evaluated. Other scenarios are heavily downlink biased. The ratio spans about five orders of magnitude, so directionality is stated per scenario.
- **Data bursts and delay sensitivity:** the traffic is bursty, periodic or not, and delay sensitive or tolerant depending on the application.
- **Round-trip delay:** important, because many AI applications are interactive. Connection and security setup add significantly to it.
- **Variability:** AI applications can vary on every dimension measured, sometimes combining different types of data in one session.
- **Tokenized traffic:** observed on the uplink where the client tokenizes media before sending it, and on the downlink wherever model output is delivered incrementally.

## Status

- TR 26.870 is at V0.6.1 (September 2026), a draft not yet approved by 3GPP. Its results are those of initial measurement campaigns, and the TR expects to align them as more results and implementations are added.
- Open points in the TR include whether and how TTFT, TTLT and tool-call latency can be used for AI traffic evaluation, and extending the token-related metrics to more scenarios and implementations.
- The testbed builds on the common test platform of TR 26.934. The TR notes that annex D should be moved into TR 26.934, and that the management of the software assets is under study.
