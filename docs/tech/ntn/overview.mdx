## How it works

NTN reuses the 5G system and the NR protocol stack; the differences are concentrated in
the radio and in a small number of architecture roles.

**Key specifications:** 3GPP
[TR 38.811](https://www.3gpp.org/dynareport/38811.htm) (Study on NR access to
non-terrestrial networks: the foundational NTN study item),
[TR 38.821](https://www.3gpp.org/dynareport/38821.htm) (Solutions for NR to
support NTN), TS 38.300 (NR overall description, NTN aspects),
[TR 38.863](https://www.3gpp.org/dynareport/38863.htm) (NTN related RF and
co-existence aspects). For satellite broadcast delivery, see also ETSI TS 103 720 (5G
Broadcast system for linear TV and radio), which is relevant to GEO-based NTN broadcast
scenarios.

### NTN system model and terminology

- **Feeder link / service link.** The service link is the wireless link between the
  NTN payload and the UE; the feeder link is the wireless transport link between the NTN
  Gateway and the NTN payload (TS 38.300 clause 3.1). To accommodate the propagation delay,
  several timing relationships are enhanced by a Common Timing Advance and configured
  offsets (TS 38.300 clause 16.14.2.1).
- **Transparent vs regenerative payload.** The NTN payload, on board a satellite or high
  altitude platform station, is a TNL node (transparent payload) or a gNB (regenerative
  payload) (TS 38.300 clause 3.1). The transparent payload forwards the radio protocol
  between the service link and the feeder link; the regenerative payload terminates the
  Uu, NG and Xn interfaces, and may terminate inter-satellite links towards other
  regenerative payloads (TS 38.300 clause 16.14.1).
- **Cells and service links.** NTN can provide Earth-moving, quasi-Earth-fixed and
  Earth-fixed cells, supported by three types of service link: Earth-fixed beams cover
  the same area all the time (e.g. GSO satellites); quasi-Earth-fixed beams cover one
  area for a limited period and another area later (e.g. NGSO satellites with steerable
  beams); Earth-moving beams slide over the Earth surface (e.g. NGSO satellites with
  fixed beams). A Tracking Area corresponds to a fixed geographical area, and a Mapped
  Cell ID corresponds to a fixed geographical area (TS 38.300 clauses 3.1 and 16.14.1).
  These cell types drive the handover models on the
  [Mobility Aspects for NTN](/tech/ntn/analysis-mobility-ntn) page.
- **GNSS at the UE.** In this release, the UE supporting NTN is GNSS-capable, and NTN
  is only applicable to FDD (TS 38.300 clause 16.14.1).

### Radio-layer adaptations for NTN

The long, variable delay and the large Doppler shift on fast NGSO links are handled
mainly at the physical and MAC layers, with assistance signalled in system information:
a large, UE-specific **timing advance** computed from GNSS position and
satellite ephemeris (plus a network-broadcast common component, anchored to an epoch
time with a validity window); **Doppler pre-compensation** on the service
link, computed by the device from geometry; a **k-offset** scheduling
offset so uplink grants and other timing relationships remain valid despite the long
round trip; and **HARQ/RRC/MAC timers** adapted for round trips that can
far exceed terrestrial values, with some HARQ processes disabled and reliability moved to
higher layers.

### NTN system information for media delivery

Two system information blocks are central to media over NTN:

- **SIB19** carries the NTN assistance information a device needs to
  acquire and track a satellite cell: serving-cell (and optionally neighbour-cell)
  ephemeris, common timing advance parameters, the k-offset, the epoch time and its
  validity duration, and the cell reference location. SIB19 was introduced with the
  Release 17 NTN work in
  [TS 38.331](https://www.3gpp.org/dynareport/38331.htm).
- **SIB27**, introduced in Release 19 of TS 38.331, contains the intended service
  areas (ISA) of MBS broadcast services in an NTN cell, each given as a polygon or a
  circle; see [MBS Broadcast NTN](/tech/ntn/analysis-mbs-broadcast-over-ntn).

### Carrying MBS over NTN

TS 23.247 Release 19 states that transmission of a Broadcast MBS Session is supported
over NR non-terrestrial networks:

- **Broadcast MBS session.** Delivered with 5GC Shared MBS traffic delivery and PTM
  delivery only, and receivable in RRC_IDLE, RRC_INACTIVE and RRC_CONNECTED. Over NR NTN,
  Release 19 adds the NR NTN Intended Service Area, carried from the 5G Core to NG-RAN in
  the MBS service area and to the UE in SIB27, which can restrict the MBS service area to
  an area smaller than a cell.
- **Multicast MBS session.** NG-RAN is the decision point for switching between PTP and
  PTM, and the UE receives it in RRC_CONNECTED, or in RRC_INACTIVE with PTM. TS 23.247
  has no NTN clause for the multicast MBS session; TS 38.321 has, since Release 17, a
  multicast DRX HARQ timer for non-terrestrial networks that adds the UE-gNB round trip.

### What each analysis page adds

- [Mobility Aspects for NTN](/tech/ntn/analysis-mobility-ntn) sets out
  three deployment models (single NTN operator; common NTN/TN operator; independent
  operators) and what Release 19 specifies for mobility in each: Earth-fixed,
  quasi-Earth-fixed and Earth-moving cells, time- and location-based conditional handover,
  the satellite switch with re-synchronization and the feeder link switchover.
- [MBS Multicast over NTN](/tech/ntn/analysis-mbs-multicast-over-ntn)
  sets out requirements for multicast over NTN, with the gNB switching each UE
  between PTP and PTM, against what Release 19 specifies.
- [Mobility for MBS Multicast over NTN](/tech/ntn/analysis-mobility-mbs-multicast-over-ntn)
  sets out what Release 19 provides, UE by UE, when multicast receivers move over NTN
  (it has no group handover), distinguishing mobility triggered by satellite motion from
  mobility triggered by the UEs.
- [MBS Broadcast over NTN](/tech/ntn/analysis-mbs-broadcast-over-ntn)
  follows the Intended Service Area of a broadcast MBS session from the 5G Core
  to SIB27, the MCCH and the UE.
